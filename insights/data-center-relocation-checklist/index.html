BUYER GUIDE / COLO-MIGO STAFF

Data Center Relocation Planning Checklist

A relocation needs more than an available cabinet and a moving date. Build a plan that connects destination readiness, network delivery, equipment transport, technical migration, and business acceptance.

IN THIS GUIDE

  1. Define the move before selecting a destination
  2. Verify facility and network readiness separately
  3. Assign transport and technical responsibilities
  4. Set go/no-go and acceptance milestones

What to leave with

A dependency schedule and responsibility list that your providers, mover, technical team, and decision owner can review together.

01 / CHECKLIST

Define the move before selecting a destination

List the origin, target markets, equipment inventory, space and power requirements, network connections, application dependencies, and intended service date. Distinguish business requirements from preferences so proposed facilities can be compared consistently.

  • Name the business owner who approves downtime and the final move.
  • Have technical specialists define allowable disruption and recovery requirements.
  • Record contract, notice, access, and exit dependencies at the existing site.
  • Identify equipment, services, or applications that require a separate migration plan.

The planning schedule should be based on confirmed dependencies, not a generic promise that every relocation can finish in a fixed number of weeks.

02 / CHECKLIST

Verify facility and network readiness separately

Ask the destination provider to confirm the specific space and usable power, installation prerequisites, access arrangements, and service delivery milestones. Give carriers and network specialists their own delivery and test milestones.

  • Confirm power and cooling fit with the responsible technical teams.
  • List circuit and cross-connect identifiers, endpoints, and required authorizations.
  • Record delivery commitments and prerequisites separately from estimates.
  • Confirm who orders, tests, accepts, and pays for each connection.

A cross-connect move may require a separate maintenance process. Equinix’s interconnection migration documentation, for example, calls for connection details, origin and destination information, and a maintenance schedule for live services. Check the selected provider’s process for your project. Read the provider example.

03 / CHECKLIST

Assign transport and technical responsibilities

Make the handoffs explicit. The selected mover performs physical transport. Assigned technical specialists own shutdown, backups and recovery checks, de-racking or installation where agreed, network changes, application testing, and rollback planning. Confirm each responsibility in the scope rather than assuming one supplier covers everything.

  • Agree packing, inventory, pickup, receiving, access, and chain-of-custody arrangements with the mover and facilities.
  • Confirm insurance and liability requirements with the appropriate advisers and suppliers.
  • Record escalation contacts and the person authorized to change the schedule.
  • Identify any gap between the mover’s scope, facility support, and technical work.

Colo-Migo can help source specialist movers and coordinate agreed milestones within a defined engagement. Colo-Migo does not itself perform physical transport or automatically take responsibility for technical migration.

04 / CHECKLIST

Set go/no-go and acceptance milestones

Before the move, have the responsible teams approve the detailed runbook, readiness evidence, test criteria, escalation path, and recovery plan. The technical plan must identify what can be reversed, by whom, and within what window; do not assume equipment transport itself is immediately reversible.

Example: if space is ready but the required circuit has not passed testing, the project is not ready simply because the truck is booked. The decision owner needs the dependency and its impact before authorizing the next milestone.

  • Record readiness evidence and the go/no-go decision.
  • Have application owners accept the agreed service tests.
  • Track any stabilization work and unresolved issues.
  • Authorize old-service termination only after acceptance and a review of remaining obligations.

Include overlapping services, carrier turn-up, moving, technical work, and exit charges in the cost comparison. An inexpensive destination can still require a substantial transition budget.
