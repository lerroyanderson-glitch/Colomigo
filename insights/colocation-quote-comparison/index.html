BUYER GUIDE / COLO-MIGO STAFF

How to Compare Colocation Quotes

Put each proposal on the same basis before choosing a provider. A useful comparison shows what is included, what remains unknown, and what must happen before service can begin.

IN THIS GUIDE

  1. Give each provider the same requirements
  2. Separate the cost categories
  3. Calculate a comparable planning total
  4. Review the conditions behind the number

What to leave with

A comparison with explicit assumptions, cost categories, delivery evidence, and unresolved questions—not a ranking based only on the advertised monthly rate.

01 / CHECKLIST

Give each provider the same requirements

Start with the same required market, space, usable power, network endpoints, growth assumptions, support expectations, and target service date. Ask providers to identify deviations instead of silently substituting a different configuration.

  • Separate must-have requirements from optional features.
  • Record what capacity is available now and what requires a future delivery milestone.
  • Have the technical owner review power units, redundancy, cooling, and network fit.
  • Use the same currency, evaluation period, and treatment of taxes for every option.

Keep the proposal version and expiration date beside each column. An old quote or a different capacity configuration is not a reliable basis for a current decision.

02 / CHECKLIST

Separate the cost categories

Break out recurring and one-time charges rather than copying a single headline price. Provider service categories illustrate why this matters: Equinix identifies physical interconnection and on-site services separately from colocation space. CoreSite also describes installation and remote hands services. Confirm what is included in your specific quote. Equinix product categories; CoreSite service overview.

  • Recurring: space, power commitments or usage, circuits, cross-connects, and support packages.
  • One-time: setup, installation, deposits where applicable, transport, and technical migration.
  • Changes over time: escalators, overages, energy adjustments, and growth assumptions.
  • Transition: old/new service overlap and confirmed exit or decommissioning costs.

Ask whether taxes, pass-through costs, minimum support hours, and other exclusions apply. Record unknown amounts as unknown rather than leaving a blank that could be mistaken for zero.

03 / CHECKLIST

Calculate a comparable planning total

For each option, add the expected charges across the same evaluation period. Apply scheduled increases to the periods they affect, rather than multiplying the opening monthly rate by the entire term. Keep any refundable deposit visible as a cash requirement, separate from a nonrefundable expense.

Planning total

Recurring charges over the evaluation period + nonrefundable one-time charges + transition costs + estimated variable charges − confirmed credits.

Label estimates, show their assumptions, and avoid counting an included service twice. If the amounts or scope are incomplete, report a partial total with the missing items listed.

Example: one proposal may include power in its quoted monthly charge while another lists it separately. Normalize the power allowance and billing basis first; otherwise the cheaper-looking figure may simply omit a required item.

04 / CHECKLIST

Review the conditions behind the number

A cost comparison should sit beside a requirements and delivery review. Have the authorized team examine term, billing commencement, renewal and notice provisions, escalators, service commitments, and remedies in the actual documents. A summary table does not replace that review.

  • Can the provider support the required configuration and timing?
  • Which dates are estimates and which are documented commitments?
  • Who owns each prerequisite, order, test, and acceptance decision?
  • Which gaps would prevent the option from meeting a must-have requirement?

Finish with a short decision memo: preferred option, rationale, unresolved questions, approval owner, and next milestone. For a move, use the relocation planning checklist. For a new term at your current site, use the renewal checklist.
